# 👤 **Social Engineering – CompTIA Network+ N10-009 – Section 4.2**

### **What is Social Engineering?**
- **Social engineering** is a technique where attackers manipulate people into breaking normal security practices, often tricking them into divulging sensitive information or allowing unauthorized access.

---

### 🔒 **Types of Social Engineering Attacks:**

#### **1. Phishing**:
- **Phishing** is when an attacker pretends to be a trusted entity (like a bank or a service provider) to steal personal information (like login credentials).
  
  #### **How it Works**:
  - The attacker sends an email that appears legitimate but contains suspicious elements, such as mismatched URLs, unusual graphics, or poor grammar.
  - The victim may be tricked into clicking a link that leads to a fake login page where their credentials are stolen.

  #### **Prevention**:
  - Always verify email senders and links by checking the URL carefully. Avoid clicking on links in unsolicited emails.

#### **2. Shoulder Surfing**:
- **Shoulder surfing** is when an attacker observes a victim’s screen in public spaces (airports, coffee shops, etc.) to gain access to sensitive information, such as passwords or financial details.

  #### **How to Protect Yourself**:
  - Be mindful of your surroundings and try to position yourself so others can't see your screen.
  - Use **privacy filters** for your screen to limit visibility to only the person directly in front of it.
  - Avoid viewing sensitive information in public.

#### **3. Tailgating & Piggybacking**:
- These techniques involve attackers gaining unauthorized physical access to a building by following an authorized person inside.

  #### **Tailgating**:
  - An attacker walks behind someone who has access to a building, hoping the door won't lock behind them.

  #### **Piggybacking**:
  - An attacker is allowed in by an authorized person, often under the guise of bringing in lunch or needing help.

  #### **Preventing Tailgating & Piggybacking**:
  - Ensure employees are trained to recognize when someone is following them in.
  - Use **visitor badges** and **strict badge-checking protocols**.
  - Implement access control systems like **airlocks** that only allow one person to enter at a time.

#### **4. Dumpster Diving**:
- **Dumpster diving** is when attackers search through an organization's trash to find valuable information, such as contact details, financial records, or passwords.

  #### **How it Works**:
  - Attackers may find sensitive documents or materials in the trash, which can help in **impersonation** or **phishing attacks**.

  #### **Prevention**:
  - **Shred sensitive documents** before disposal.
  - **Lock dumpsters** and install security cameras to prevent unauthorized access to trash.
  - Consider using third-party **shredding services** for confidential waste.

---

### 🛡️ **General Prevention Tips**:
1. **Awareness**: Stay vigilant of suspicious behaviors and constantly educate staff about the risks of social engineering.
2. **Training**: Regularly conduct training to help employees identify phishing emails, unauthorized access attempts, and other common social engineering tactics.
3. **Physical Security**: Always control access to physical spaces with badges, locks, and secure entry systems.
4. **Data Disposal**: Properly dispose of sensitive information by shredding documents and locking up garbage bins.

---

### 🚨 **Key Takeaways**:
1. **Phishing**: Fake emails or websites designed to steal your information. Always verify sources before clicking on links.
2. **Shoulder Surfing**: Attackers can view your screen in public. Use privacy filters and be aware of your surroundings.
3. **Tailgating/Piggybacking**: Attackers gain unauthorized access by following or being allowed in by an authorized person. Always monitor building entry points and implement strict access control procedures.
4. **Dumpster Diving**: Attackers may search through trash for sensitive information. Shred important documents and secure your trash bins.
