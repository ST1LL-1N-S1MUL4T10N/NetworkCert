# ⚡ Power – Network+ (N10-009) – Section 2.4

### 🔌 Electrical Safety
- **Safety First**: Always ensure you’re disconnected from power before working with electrical devices. Be cautious even with devices not connected to power as they may store charges (e.g., capacitors in CRT monitors, laser printers).
- **Avoid Contact**: Never touch energized components, and always keep safety in mind.

---

### 🔋 Electrical Measurements and Terms
- **Ampere (A)**: Measures the rate of electron flow (similar to water flowing through a hose). The higher the amps, the more current can flow.
- **Voltage (V)**: Measures the pressure of electricity (similar to water pressure in the hose). Voltage can vary depending on the region:
  - **US/Canada**: 110-120V, 60Hz
  - **Europe**: 220-240V, 50Hz
- **Power (Watts)**: Calculated as **Volts × Amps** (e.g., 120V × 0.5A = 60W).

---

### ⚡ Power Types
- **Alternating Current (AC)**: Used for long-distance distribution (e.g., wall outlets). Fluctuates in direction and is represented by a wavy line.
- **Direct Current (DC)**: Used by devices like routers and switches, providing a constant direction and voltage. AC is converted to DC by devices’ power supplies.

---

### 🔌 Power Protection: UPS
- **UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply)**: Provides backup power when there’s an outage and smooths voltage fluctuations (brownouts and surges).
  - **Offline or Standby UPS**: Waits for power loss before kicking in, with a small time gap (may cause device reboots).
  - **Line-Interactive UPS**: Compensates for voltage drops (brownouts) and keeps power consistent.
  - **Online or Double-Conversion UPS**: Always runs from battery power and keeps devices protected with no interruptions, even when power is lost.

---

### 🔋 UPS Features
- **Battery Management**: Some UPS units can automatically shut down devices if the battery is low.
- **Battery Capacity**: Varies by UPS model, and higher capacity units offer longer backup times.
- **Outlets and Connections**: Different models have various outlets and sometimes additional ports for phone or cable connections.

---

### 🔌 Power Distribution Units (PDU)
- **PDU**: A power distribution unit that manages multiple devices in a data center or rack. PDUs are more advanced than basic surge protectors, allowing remote management, device power cycling, and monitoring.
  - **Remote Management**: PDUs can be accessed via the web to control devices, configure settings, and monitor power usage.

---

### 🛠️ Key Takeaways:
1. **Safety**: Always respect electricity and ensure devices are disconnected from power sources before handling.
2. **Electrical Terms**: Understand **amps**, **volts**, and **watts** for better electrical comprehension.
3. **Power Types**: AC is used for distribution, while DC is used by most devices. Conversion occurs inside power supplies.
4. **UPS**: Protects against power outages and fluctuations. Different types cater to varying levels of backup power needs.
5. **PDU**: Advanced power management tool that allows remote control and monitoring in data centers.
